# Acute Crisis Escalation & Referral Protocol for Nigerian PHCs

## 1. Purpose & Triage Thresholds
This protocol establishes definitive operational steps for Primary Healthcare Centre (PHC) staff, nurses, and Community Health Extension Workers (CHEWs) when encountering patients in psychiatric or neuropsychiatric crisis.

### Alert Classification
- **CODE RED (Immediate Emergency - Zero Delay)**:
  - Active suicide attempt or imminent suicide intent with a plan/means (e.g., patient holds sniper/pesticide, sharp object, or has ligature marks).
  - Violent agitation, acute mania, or aggressive psychosis endangering self or others.
  - Acute severe delirium (fever + confusion + combative state).
  - Suspected acute poisoning or severe toxidrome (organophosphates, tramadol overdose, alcohol poisoning).
  - Status epilepticus or repeated seizures.
- **CODE YELLOW (Urgent - Review within 2 Hours)**:
  - Severe depression with expressed death wishes ("life don tire me" / "gwara in mutu da wannan rayuwa") without an immediate executable plan.
  - Acute hallucinations or paranoid beliefs without immediate physical aggression.
  - Suspected postpartum psychosis or severe depression in new mothers.
  - Mild-to-moderate alcohol or benzodiazepine/tramadol withdrawal without delirium.
- **CODE GREEN (Routine Care)**:
  - Chronic mild anxiety, insomnia, stress from socioeconomic hardship, chronic managed mental disorders requesting medication refills.

## 2. Emergency Suicide & Self-Harm Interventions (CODE RED)

### A. Immediate Action Steps
1. **Remove Lethal Means**: Immediately scan and remove sharp implements, medications, cords/ropes, chemical bottles (especially agrochemical pesticides like *Sniper* or Paraquat).
2. **Continuous Visual Observation**: The patient MUST NOT be left alone under any circumstances—not in waiting areas, triage rooms, or latrines. Assign a designated health worker or responsible family member to remain within arm's reach.
3. **Calm De-escalation**:
   - Speak in a calm, non-judgmental tone.
   - Do not preach, condemn, or argue moral/religious prohibitions against suicide.
   - Validate emotional pain: *"I hear how painful this is for you right now. We are here to help you get through this."* / *"Na hear say body don tire you, but make we hold hand together help you."*
4. **Physical and Medical Stabilization**:
   - If poison was ingested: Check airway, breathing, circulation (ABC). Record time and bottle name if available. Do not induce vomiting if caustic or petroleum distillate. Administer emergency supportive care (oxygen, IV access).
   - If hanging/strangulation: Secure cervical spine; assess airway patency.

## 3. Acute Psychosis & Aggressive Agitation De-escalation Protocol

### A. Non-Violent De-escalation (Universal First-Line)
1. **Physical Spacing**: Maintain at least 2 arm lengths of space between yourself and the patient. Never corner the patient or block their exit route. Ensure the healthcare worker always has an unhindered exit path.
2. **Environmental Control**: Minimize noise, turn down bright lights, and request non-essential bystanders and crowd members to step outside.
3. **Communication Stance**:
   - Keep hands open and visible. Avoid crossed arms or sudden hand movements.
   - Speak in short, concise, respectful sentences.
   - Do NOT confront or mock hallucinations or delusions:
     - *Incorrect*: "You are lying, nobody is in the roof."
     - *Correct*: "I believe that you hear those voices, and I see they make you afraid. You are safe here with us in the clinic."

### B. Medical Management (Supervised PHC Medical Officer or CHEW protocol)
- If the patient is uncontrollably combative and poses immediate risk of severe harm after verbal de-escalation fails:
  - Consult the attending Medical Officer or call the referral hospital.
  - Where authorized by national/state PHC clinical standing orders, parenteral sedation (e.g., Haloperidol 2.5mg-5mg IM or Diazepam 5mg-10mg slow IV/IM) must only be administered by trained licensed providers with continuous vital sign monitoring (pulse oximetry and respiratory rate).
  - Physical restraints must be avoided if possible; if temporarily used for medical transfer, use soft padded ties, never chains, and check limb circulation every 15 minutes.

## 4. Referral & Handover Pathway in Nigeria

```
Level 1: Community / PHC (Triage, First Aid, De-escalation, mRDT, Vital Signs)
      │
      ▼ (Code Red / Severe Code Yellow)
Level 2: General Hospital / District Hospital (Emergency Room, Medical Officer, IV access)
      │
      ▼ (Specialist / Refractory Psychiatric Care)
Level 3: Federal Neuropsychiatric Hospital (FNPH) / State Teaching Hospital
```

### Standard Escort and Transfer Protocol
1. **Accompanying Personnel**: For all Code Red transfers, a trained CHEW, community health officer (CHO), or registered nurse must accompany the patient in the ambulance or transport vehicle alongside at least one close family elder or guardian.
2. **Transfer Clinical Handover Note**:
   - Patient Name, Age, Gender, Primary Village/Town, Next of Kin phone number.
   - Initial presenting complaint (include direct patient quotes, whether in Hausa, Pidgin, or English).
   - Vital signs recorded over time (BP, pulse, SpO2, temperature, blood glucose).
   - Interventions given at the PHC (medications administered, dosage, time, IV fluids).
   - Rapid test results (e.g., Malaria mRDT: Positive/Negative, Urine dipstick, Blood sugar).
   - Reason for transfer: Specific psychiatric emergency requiring secondary/tertiary specialist intervention.
