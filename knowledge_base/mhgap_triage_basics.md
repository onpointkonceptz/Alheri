# WHO mhGAP Primary Care & Mental Health Triage Protocol (Nigeria Adaptation)

## 1. Overview & Clinical Objective
This clinical triage guide adapts the World Health Organization Mental Health Gap Action Programme (mhGAP) Intervention Guide (Version 2.0) for primary healthcare clinics (PHCs) and community health extension workers (CHEWs) in low-resource settings across Nigeria. 

The primary goal is early identification, risk stratification, basic non-pharmacological support, medical rule-out, and urgent referral for priority mental, neurological, and substance use (MNS) disorders.

---

## 2. Priority Conditions & Clinical Presentations

### A. Depressive Conditions (Bacin Ciki / Low Mood / Body Heaviness)
- **Core Symptoms**: Persistent sadness or irritability; loss of interest in usual activities (*rashin sha'awa*); marked fatigue and low energy (*kasala / rashin kuzari*).
- **Associated Symptoms**: Disturbed sleep (initial insomnia or early morning awakening); appetite and weight changes; feelings of worthlessness or guilt; difficulty concentrating; medically unexplained somatic pain (headaches, general body heat, chest tightness).
- **Assessment Questions**:
  - *"In the past 2 weeks, have you felt sad, down, or hopeless almost every day?"*
  - *"Have you lost interest or joy in things you normally enjoy?"*

### B. Anxiety & Stress-Related Conditions (Damuwa / Tension / Restlessness)
- **Clinical Signs**: Pervasive worry, trembling, dry mouth, sweating, rapid heartbeat (*bugun zuciya*), hyperventilation, gastrointestinal disturbances.
- **Panic Presentation**: Acute episodes of intense fear, chest tightness, choking sensation, fear of dying or losing control.

### C. Psychosis & Bipolar Conditions (Hauka / Tabin Hankali / Acute Confusion)
- **Clinical Signs**: Hallucinations (hearing voices when no one is around); delusions (fixed beliefs of persecution, spiritual attacks, grandiosity); disorganized speech; erratic, hostile, or withdrawn behavior.
- **Differential**: Always rule out organic delirium, cerebral malaria, meningitis, or substance toxicity before diagnosing primary psychiatric illness.

### D. Substance Use & Prescription Misuse
- **Prevalent Substances**: Alcohol, cannabis, codeine-containing cough syrup, tramadol, Rohypnol.
- **Signs of Acute Intoxication/Withdrawal**: Dilated/constricted pupils, slurred speech, ataxia, tremors, diaphoresis, autonomic instability, seizures (especially tramadol or alcohol withdrawal).

---

## 3. Physical & Organic Rule-Outs in Nigerian Primary Care
Before attributing symptoms solely to psychiatric illness, CHEWs and primary clinicians must rule out or treat organic medical emergencies:
1. **Malaria / Cerebral Malaria**: Rapid Diagnostic Test (mRDT) or blood smear for any patient with fever, altered sensorium, chills, or headache.
2. **Typhoid Psychosis**: Sepsis with high step-ladder fever, abdominal tenderness, delirium, or toxic encephalopathy.
3. **Severe Anemia**: Pallor of conjunctiva and palms, tachycardia, extreme fatigue often presenting as depression.
4. **Hypoglycemia**: Fingerstick blood glucose check; tremors, sweating, confusion, irritability.
5. **Postpartum Complications**: Postpartum psychosis or severe puerperal depression occurring within 6 weeks post-delivery. Immediate medical and psychiatric escalation required.
6. **Epilepsy / Seizures**: Distinguish post-ictal confusion from acute psychotic episodes.

## 4. Triage Stratification & Action Plan

| Priority Level | Clinical Presentation | Clinical Action |
|---|---|---|
| **RED (Emergency)** | Imminent suicide attempt, severe violence/agitation, acute poisoning/overdose, status epilepticus, delirium with high fever. | Immediate vital stabilization. Ensure patient safety in a quiet room. Never leave unattended. Urgent transfer to Secondary/Tertiary hospital with trained escort. |
| **YELLOW (Urgent)** | Severe depression with passive death wishes, active hallucinations without acute aggression, moderate alcohol/tramadol withdrawal, marked functional impairment. | Dedicated CHEW/MO evaluation within 2 hours. Initiate supportive listening, psychoeducation. Rule out organic illness. Involve trusted family member. Plan specialist referral within 24-48 hours. |
| **GREEN (Routine)** | Mild-to-moderate depressive/anxiety symptoms, chronic stable epilepsy, grief reaction, sleep hygiene complaints. | Outpatient PHC counseling, problem-solving techniques, stress management, sleep hygiene, schedule 2-week follow-up at the clinic. |

---

## 5. Non-Pharmacological Communication Guidelines
1. **Active Listening**: Do not dismiss cultural explanations of distress. Acknowledge the patient's pain.
2. **Safe Environment**: Maintain eye contact, speak in calm and gentle tones, sit at eye level.
3. **Involve Support Network**: With patient consent, identify a reliable family elder or caregiver for follow-up support.
4. **Confidentiality**: Reassure the patient that all disclosures remain strictly confidential within the clinical team.
